The final artefact I have created and designed is one I am frankly more proud of than any project I would have made on the microbit, regardless of how overengineered I would have made it.

My system that didn't exist in November now collects data to a very high standard, emails me with program alerts as well as weather alerts, automatically restarts itself if something crashes, or in the event of power-loss, all while complying to standard practices in the IT industry.

If a company requests an example of a project I have made relating to the careers I am looking into progressing towards, I would currently show them this.

The Simulation model is also made very well, in my opinion, as it theoretically can use any CSV file with rain, wind, temperature and humidity values that has a length of longer than 3 days for minimum results, or 1 year for minimum recommended results. I haven't tested this program on data longer than 16 years, but considering 16 years of data results in nearly 300,000 individual points of data (if I remember correctly), I would have faith in it.


As much as I will praise my project, I am a perfectionist and I always see ways I could improve on this project, and plan on improving after I get my leaving certificate results in August.


  1. The embedded system has some good hardware, but I want to make a 3D-printed housing for the Pi, a PCB for all the hardware, and the sensors to be in a separate housing, connected via RJ45 cable for ease-of-deployment

  2. The program could also have a user choice of which what-if scenarios an end-user wanted to see, possibly with user-inputted values, but I became aware that my project is already over-the-top.
